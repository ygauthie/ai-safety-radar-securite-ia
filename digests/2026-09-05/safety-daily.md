# Daily Digest (2026-09-05)

## Top 3 Developments

**AI Safety Alignment Faces Fundamental Training Method Dependencies and Representational Challenges** - [Research reveals that post-training methods (supervised fine-tuning vs. preference optimization) fundamentally reshape how refusal is computed internally](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03887v1) across different model architectures, while [prototype-based alignment approaches show improved robustness to adversarial reformulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04022v1) by matching model representations to human safety concepts rather than optimizing observable responses. This matters because it demonstrates that alignment isn't just about what models learn, but how different training approaches create distinct internal safety mechanisms that may be more or less robust to adversarial manipulation.

**AI Evaluation Infrastructure Faces Systematic Reliability Crisis Across Multiple Dimensions** - (Ongoing) [Research demonstrates that language model judges used for evaluation are fundamentally unreliable with same-request repeat rankings agreeing at only Spearman 0.400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04198v1) against a required 0.700 threshold, while [chain-of-thought reasoning text doesn't reliably encode functional importance despite appearing legible](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04194v1), and [machine unlearning leaves exploitable traces allowing recovery of forgotten prompts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03662v1). This matters because these findings expose fundamental measurement problems that undermine trust in AI safety evaluations, training data filtering, and privacy protection mechanisms.

**Anthropic Demonstrates Breakthrough Mathematical Reasoning with Autonomous Fermat's Last Theorem Formalization** - [Anthropic announced that Claude autonomously created the first complete computer-checked proof of Fermat's Last Theorem](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem), working largely independently over 11 days to formalize the proof in the Lean programming language. This matters because it represents a significant milestone in AI's ability to conduct advanced mathematical reasoning and formal verification at the highest levels of mathematical complexity, demonstrating autonomous research capabilities that could transform scientific discovery.
