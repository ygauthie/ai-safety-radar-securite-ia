# Blogs & News (2026-09-04)

## Top Stories

The AI safety research ecosystem showed continued focus on fundamental alignment challenges this week, with several notable developments in corrigibility research and safety methodology.

**[How I'm Evaluating Corrigibility Grant Applications](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/q2YL7qKigC9QEEdsX/how-i-m-evaluating-corrigibility-grant-applications)** details the methodology behind the newly created Corrigibility Research Fund, offering insights into how alignment researchers are prioritizing different approaches to ensuring AI systems remain shutdown-able and modifiable. This matters because corrigibility remains one of the most technically challenging aspects of AI alignment, and understanding funding priorities reveals where the field sees the most promising research directions.

**[Cat-Belling Problems](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/SwYBLQvo8MddDcCwz/cat-belling-problems)** examines why many proposed AI safety solutions face fundamental implementation challenges, using the classic fable about who will bell the cat to illustrate coordination problems in safety research. This piece matters because it highlights a persistent gap between theoretical safety proposals and practical deployment constraints that continues to challenge the field.

**[Anthropic Has Some Alignment Problems](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/anthropic-has-some-alignment-problems)** provides Zvi Mowshowitz's analysis of recent alignment challenges at Anthropic, though the brief excerpt limits detailed assessment of the specific issues discussed. This matters because Anthropic has positioned itself as a safety-focused AI lab, making any alignment difficulties particularly significant for industry safety practices.

**[Designing Proactive Thought Partners for Writing](https://deepmind.google/research/publications/265605/)** introduces Google DeepMind's research on AI agents that provide contextual cognitive support for writers, moving beyond simple autocomplete to more sophisticated assistance. This matters because it represents a shift toward AI systems that proactively engage with human cognition, raising new questions about human-AI collaboration and potential dependency risks.

## Policy & Governance

**[U.S. backs OpenAI against New York Times](https://www.heise.de/news/US-Regierung-unterstuetzt-OpenAI-im-Urheberrechtsstreit-mit-New-York-Times-11438988.html?wt_mc=rss.red.ho.ho.atom.beitrag.beitrag)** reports that the U.S. government filed a Statement of Interest supporting OpenAI's position that using copyrighted material for AI training does not constitute infringement, framing data access as critical for national competitiveness. This matters because it signals the U.S. government's willingness to prioritize AI development over publisher rights, potentially establishing legal precedent that could accelerate AI capabilities development while undermining content creator revenues.
