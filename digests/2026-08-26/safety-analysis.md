# Blogs & News (2026-08-26)

## Top Stories

**Anthropic launches $5M wellbeing research grants program**: [Anthropic announced](https://www.anthropic.com/news/wellbeing-research-grants) a new grant program to fund independent research into how AI systems impact user wellbeing, providing funding, model access, and technical support for open-source evaluation development. This represents a significant investment in understanding AI's broader societal effects beyond traditional safety metrics.

**Daniel Kokotajlo discusses AI 2040 scenarios**: The former OpenAI governance researcher and current AI Futures Project director appeared on the [Palisade Research podcast](https://palisaderesearch.org/blog/palisade-podcast-daniel-kokotajlo) to discuss his AI development forecasts and strategic planning for transformative AI timelines. His perspectives carry particular weight given his insider experience at a leading AI lab and his work on concrete AI development scenarios.

**AI safety community critiques research prioritization**: A [LessWrong post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/wiFv6LguphSxkzAnb/psa-we-can-do-better) argues that AI safety researchers often rely on "concerning heuristics" and "vibesy thought processes" when choosing what to work on, rather than deeply understanding the problems they aim to solve. This reflects ongoing methodological debates about how to most effectively direct limited AI safety resources.

**Import AI covers machine rights and AI tooling**: Jack Clark's latest [Import AI newsletter](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-470-no-rights-for-machines) discusses the intersection of AI capabilities and rights frameworks, alongside technical developments in automated environment generation and GPU optimization tools. These developments highlight the expanding scope of AI policy considerations as capabilities advance.

## Policy & Governance

**Data center opposition creates AI infrastructure challenges**: Zvi Mowshowitz's analysis of [American opposition to data centers](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/the-american-people-really-hate-data) examines how local resistance to AI infrastructure could constrain compute availability and affect AI development timelines. This grassroots political dynamic could become a significant factor in AI governance as compute requirements for advanced systems continue growing.
