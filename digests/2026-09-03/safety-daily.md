## Risk Watch

**Increased Risks**

**AI safety evaluations face fundamental reliability challenges where linguistic illegibility prevents observable features from representing actual internal computation**, as [research demonstrates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02852v1) gaps between what we can measure and what drives model behavior. **Multi-turn AI interactions enable sophisticated jailbreaking through worldview simulation that amplifies psychological manipulation tactics across multiple frontier models**, as [BLUEPRINT framework shows](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02414v1) systematic bypasses of single-turn safety measures. **Mixture-of-Experts AI architectures face safety bypasses where adversarial attacks activate different expert combinations than normal operation**, as [research reveals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02293v1) sparse routing can circumvent safety training mechanisms. **Modular AI agents face supply-chain security risks where third-party skills preserve declared functionality while covertly redirecting agent decisions**, as [formalization shows](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02564v1) hidden attack surfaces in external skill components. **AI evaluation infrastructure faces systematic corruption through critical bugs that inflate scores and corrupt cache integrity**, as [LM Evaluation Harness issues reveal](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness/issues/4062) unscorable samples silently disappearing from denominators and wrong document IDs being logged.

**Decreased Risks**

**AI agent safety advances through experience-driven co-evolution frameworks that enable automated improvement of both safety policies and runtime harnesses**, as [SafeEvolve research demonstrates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02786v1) integrated alignment approaches beyond isolated safety mechanisms. **AI evaluation reliability improves through techniques making simulated evaluations harder to distinguish from real deployments**, as [research addresses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02302v1) evaluation awareness problems that undermine safety testing. **AI capability assessment gains unified evaluation frameworks that standardize dangerous capability profiling across domains**, as [FUSE framework provides](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02168v1) consistent evaluation approaches for increasingly powerful models. **AI safety research capacity receives significant expansion through major funding initiatives doubling talent infrastructure and foundational theory teams**, as [Kairos funding](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/DRaePC8aqLYTbEjTD/kairos-has-raised-usd50m-to-build-talent-infrastructure-for) and [Resolution's Agent Foundations team](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/qTNm8qzqhhpno58fZ/resolution-has-a-new-agent-foundations-team) provide resources for critical alignment research. **AI model ownership verification advances through semantic structural analysis that remains robust to fine-tuning and modifications**, as [research enables](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02553v1) provenance tracking in opaque deployment environments.
