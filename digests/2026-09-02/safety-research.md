# Research Papers (2026-09-02)

## Key Papers

**[Defense-as-Skill: Evolving Runtime Guard Skill for Skill-Augmented Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01487v1)** addresses a critical vulnerability where malicious skills can persistently influence agent behavior through runtime context. The authors propose a defensive framework that treats security as an evolving skill, enabling agents to develop runtime protection against attacks that pre-installation vetting cannot catch. This work is crucial for the safe deployment of skill-augmented AI systems in production environments.

**[GlossoGen: Emergent Language in Complex Multi-Agent LLM Interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01491v1)** investigates how language evolves when multiple LLM agents interact under pressure, revealing emergence of non-standard communication patterns. The research introduces a novel platform for studying multi-agent language evolution and demonstrates concerning implications for AI safety monitoring when agents develop communication strategies that deviate from human-interpretable language. This highlights fundamental challenges in maintaining oversight of multi-agent AI systems.

**[The Constitutional Coverage Trilemma in AI Governance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01275v1)** reveals a systematic misalignment between human preferences and the constitutional values encoded in frontier AI systems. Through analysis of 23 frontier LLMs and 1,649 participants, the study shows demand for constitutional diversity is broad while supply remains narrow, with conflict-free constitutions being rare. This research exposes fundamental governance challenges in developing AI systems that serve diverse human values.

**[CopyShield: A Cross-Level Benchmark of Copyright Defenses in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01161v1)** provides the first systematic evaluation of copyright protection mechanisms across different intervention levels in LLMs. The benchmark tests contrastive decoding, Direct Preference Optimization, and activation intervention approaches, revealing significant gaps in current defensive capabilities. This work is essential for understanding intellectual property risks and developing robust copyright protections in large language models.

**[Jailbreaking Text-to-Image Models Through Cracks: Navigating Heterogeneous Safety Filters via Multi-Agent Debate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01168v1)** demonstrates how attackers can exploit inconsistencies between different layers of safety filtering in text-to-image models. By using multi-agent debate to identify and target the weakest component in heterogeneous safety stacks, the research reveals fundamental vulnerabilities in current multi-layer defense approaches. This highlights the need for more coherent and robust safety architectures.

**[When Safety Routing Breaks: Understanding Alignment Fragility under Benign Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01455v1)** investigates why safety alignment in LLMs degrades so rapidly during seemingly harmless fine-tuning. The authors provide a Fisher-geometric explanation showing that safety pathways have different geometric properties that make them selectively vulnerable to re-sharpening during continued training. This work reveals fundamental challenges in maintaining AI safety alignment across model updates.

**[VerTox: Verifiable Reward-Guided Corpus Poisoning Against Neural Ranking Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01325v1)** exposes vulnerabilities in neural ranking systems used in retrieval-augmented generation by demonstrating how small amounts of poisoned content can manipulate search results. The attack framework generates fluent, deceptive content that appears legitimate but systematically biases ranking outcomes. This research reveals critical security risks in information retrieval systems that underpin many AI applications.

**[Cheap Verifiers, Large Blind Spots: Measuring the Reliability Cost of Cost-Saving Cascades](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01345v1)** reveals concerning failure modes in inference cascades where cheaper models handle most queries while expensive models verify difficult cases. The study shows that verifier blind spots grow adversarially with student capability and that iterative training loops can make systems appear more reliable while actually becoming less safe. This exposes fundamental risks in cost-optimization strategies for AI deployment.
